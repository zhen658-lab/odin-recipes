Klepon (Pandan Rice Cakes with Palm Sugar)

Description

Klepon is a beloved traditional Indonesian snack made from glutinous rice flour, colored and scented with fresh pandan. Each soft, chewy ball hides a small piece of palm sugar that melts as it cooks, then bursts open with warm, caramel-like sweetness when you bite into it. The cakes are rolled in lightly salted grated coconut, which balances the sweetness. Klepon is a classic jajanan pasar (traditional market snack) and is often enjoyed with tea.

Ingredients

  • 200 grams glutinous rice flour
  • 0.3 teaspoons salt (for the dough)
  • 10 pandan leaves, cut into pieces
  • 150 milliliters water, for blending the pandan leaves
  • 100 grams palm sugar (gula aren), finely chopped
  • 150 grams freshly grated coconut (young to semi-mature)
  • 0.3 teaspoons salt (for the coconut)
  • 1.5 liters water, for boiling the cakes

Steps

  1. Make the pandan juice: Blend 10 pandan leaves, cut into pieces with 150 milliliters water, for blending the pandan leaves until fine. Strain through a cloth or fine sieve, pressing to extract as much green juice as possible. You should get about 120–150 ml of juice.
  2. Make the dough: Mix 200 grams glutinous rice flour and 0.3 teaspoons salt (for the dough) in a bowl. Gradually add the pandan juice while kneading, until you have a soft, smooth dough that does not crack or stick to your hands. Add a little more water if it feels dry, or a little more flour if it is too sticky.
  3. Steam the coconut: Mix 150 grams freshly grated coconut (young to semi-mature) with 0.3 teaspoons salt (for the coconut) and steam in a steamer so it stays fresh longer and tastes fuller. Set aside to cool.
  4. Fill and shape: Take about 15 g of dough and flatten it in your palm. Place a small piece of 100 grams palm sugar (gula aren), finely chopped in the center, fold the dough over, and seal well, then roll into a smooth ball. Make sure there are no gaps, or the sugar will leak out while boiling.
  5. Boil: Bring 1.5 liters water, for boiling the cakes to a rolling boil. Drop in the cakes a few at a time. When they float to the surface, cook them 1–2 more minutes so the filling melts completely.
  6. Drain and coat: Remove with a slotted spoon, drain briefly, then roll each cake in the steamed coconut until fully coated. Serve warm or at room temperature.

Notes

this recipe makes about 24 pieces (roughly 15 g of dough each), enough for 6–8 people as a snack at 3–4 pieces per person. The servings control above counts pieces, so set it to 12 for a half batch or 48 for a double batch. This is a home-kitchen adaptation; klepon varies by region and family, for example in the sweetness of the filling or whether the coconut is steamed. Warning: the melted sugar inside is very hot, so let the cakes cool a little before eating. Klepon is best eaten the same day; the coconut coating dries out, and the cakes harden if left too long.
