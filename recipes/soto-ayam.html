Soto Ayam (Indonesian Turmeric Chicken Soup)

Description

Soto ayam is one of Indonesia’s most loved comfort foods. This traditional chicken soup is known for its golden, fragrant broth, made with turmeric, lemongrass, galangal, and a blend of aromatic spices. Each bowl is filled with tender shredded chicken, vermicelli, crisp bean sprouts, and boiled egg, then finished with fried shallots and a squeeze of lime. Found everywhere from street stalls to family dining tables, soto comes in many regional versions across the archipelago, and it is usually served with steamed rice or lontong and a spoonful of spicy sambal.

Ingredients

  • 800 grams bone-in chicken pieces (thighs and drumsticks)
  • 2 liters water for the broth
  • 6 shallots,peeled
  • 5 garlic cloves,peeled
  • 3 candlenuts(or macadamia nuts)
  • 20 grams fresh turmeric,peeled
  • 15 grams fresh ginger,peeled
  • 0.5 teaspoons ground white pepper
  • 1 teaspoons ground coriander
  • 2 tablespoons vegetable oil
  • 2 lemongrass stalks,bruised
  • 20 grams galangal,bruised
  • 2 Indonesian bay leaves (daun salam)
  • 3 kaffir lime leaves
  • 2 teaspoons salt,adjust to taste
  • 100 grams rice vermicelli (soun)
  • 100 grams bean sprouts
  • 4 hard-boiled eggs,halved
  • 3 tablespoons fried shallots,for topping
  • 2 limes, cut into wedges

Steps

  1. Boil the chicken: Bring 2 liters water for the broth to a boil in a large pot. Add 800 grams bone-in chicken pieces (thighs and drumsticks) and skim off the foam that rises to the top. Reduce the heat and simmer until the chicken is cooked through. Remove the chicken and keep the broth in the pot. Once cool enough to handle, shred the meat and discard the bones, or keep them in the broth for more flavor.
  2. Make the spice paste: Blend 6 shallots, peeled, 5 garlic cloves, peeled, 3 candlenuts (or macadamia nuts), 20 grams fresh turmeric, peeled, 15 grams fresh ginger, peeled, 0.5 teaspoons ground white pepper, and 1 teaspoons ground coriander with a few tablespoons of the broth into a smooth paste.
  3. Sauté the spices: Heat 2 tablespoons vegetable oil in a pan over medium heat. Cook the spice paste with 2 lemongrass stalks, bruised, 20 grams galangal, bruised, 2 Indonesian bay leaves (daun salam), and 3 kaffir lime leaves until fragrant and the raw smell is gone.
  4. Simmer the broth: Stir the sautéed spices into the broth and add 2 teaspoons salt, adjust to taste. Simmer gently so the flavors blend. Taste and adjust the seasoning, then remove the lemongrass, galangal, and leaves if you like.
  5. Prepare the vermicelli and sprouts: Soak 100 grams rice vermicelli (soun) in hot water until soft, then drain. Briefly blanch 100 grams bean sprouts in boiling water for about 30 seconds and drain 5 minutes
  6. Assemble and serve: Place vermicelli and bean sprouts in each bowl, add shredded chicken and half a hard-boiled egg (4 hard-boiled eggs, halved), then ladle in the hot broth. Top with 3 tablespoons fried shallots, for topping and serve with 2 limes, cut into wedges and sambal on the side. Serve with steamed rice or lontong (compressed rice cake).

Notes

this recipe makes about 6 bowls (about 2 liters of broth), enough for 6 people as a main dish. The servings control above counts bowls, so set it to 3 for a half batch or 12 for a double batch. This is a home-kitchen adaptation; soto has many regional versions across Indonesia (such as Soto Lamongan, Soto Betawi, and Soto Banjar) that differ in broth, spices, and toppings. Fresh turmeric can be replaced with 1 teaspoon of ground turmeric if needed. The broth tastes even better the next day; store it in the fridge for up to 2 days and reheat until boiling before serving.
